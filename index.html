Profile

I am a geophysicist with a PhD in Geology from the University of Exeter and research and teaching experience across geophysics, seismology, volcanology, and geophysical data science. My work centres on understanding Earth processes through physics-based numerical modelling, geodetic and seismic data analysis, and uncertainty-aware interpretation.

I develop coupled thermo-poroelastic and poroviscoelastic finite-element models, principally in COMSOL Multiphysics, to investigate volcanic unrest, magma-reservoir stability, melt migration, and eruptive transitions. Soufrière Hills Volcano, Montserrat, is my primary case study, with Sakurajima, Japan, as a comparative system. I integrate InSAR, GNSS, seismic, and petrological observations into these models.

My earlier research used seismic-array methods and joint inversions to study earthquake site response and liquefaction hazard in Kazakhstan. Alongside research, I have four years of university teaching and student-support experience, six first-author peer-reviewed publications, and a record of collaborative, reproducible computational work.
